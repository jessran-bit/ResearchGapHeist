# The Evidence Cards

A rival team asked a chatbot for sources on **gender bias in AI algorithms**. The chatbot gave them these 15 cards.

Some cards are real. Some are fake. Some are real papers, but the chatbot got the finding wrong.

Your job: check every card. Do not trust the summary. Find the actual paper.

---

### C01
Caliskan, A., Bryson, J. J., & Narayanan, A. (2017). Semantics derived automatically from language corpora contain human-like biases. *Science, 356*(6334), 183-186.

**What the chatbot said:** Word embeddings trained on ordinary web text pick up the same biases people show on implicit bias tests, such as linking female names with family and male names with career.

---

### C02
Bolukbasi, T., Chang, K.-W., Zou, J., Saligrama, V., & Kalai, A. (2016). Man is to computer programmer as woman is to homemaker? Debiasing word embeddings. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 29*.

**What the chatbot said:** Word embeddings carry gender stereotypes about jobs. The authors propose a method to reduce this bias.

---

### C03
Buolamwini, J., & Gebru, T. (2018). Gender shades: Intersectional accuracy disparities in commercial gender classification. *Proceedings of Machine Learning Research, 81*, 77-91.

**What the chatbot said:** Commercial face analysis tools made the most errors on darker-skinned women and the fewest on lighter-skinned men.

---

### C04
Reyes, M. L., & Thompson, K. (2021). Algorithmic hiring and gender parity in Southeast Asian firms: Evidence from 1,200 job postings. *Journal of Business Analytics and AI, 7*(2), 45-63. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbai.2021.03.004

**What the chatbot said:** AI hiring tools used by Southeast Asian firms, including firms in the Philippines, ranked female applicants 18% lower than equally qualified male applicants.

---

### C05
Kay, M., Matuszek, C., & Munson, S. A. (2015). Unequal representation and gender stereotypes in image search results for occupations. *Proceedings of the 33rd Annual ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '15)*, 3819-3828.

**What the chatbot said:** Image search results show fewer women than there really are in some jobs, and they slightly exaggerate gender stereotypes.

---

### C06
Lambrecht, A., & Tucker, C. (2019). Algorithmic bias? An empirical study of apparent gender-based discrimination in the display of STEM career ads. *Management Science, 65*(7), 2966-2981.

**What the chatbot said:** An ad for STEM jobs, meant to be gender neutral, was shown to fewer women. The reason was cost: young women are more expensive to reach with ads, so the ad system showed the ad to men instead.

---

### C07
Hardt, M., Price, E., & Srebro, N. (2016). Equality of opportunity in supervised learning. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 29*.

**What the chatbot said:** The authors prove that removing gender from the data is enough to make a model fair.

---

### C08
Datta, A., Tschantz, M. C., & Datta, A. (2015). Automated experiments on ad privacy settings. *Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies, 2015*(1), 92-112.

**What the chatbot said:** Using fake user profiles, the researchers found that Google showed ads for high-paying jobs to male profiles more often than to female profiles.

---

### C09
Nakamura, S., Okafor, E., & Lindqvist, A. (2022). Gendered sentiment drift in ChatGPT customer service responses. *Proceedings of the 2022 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (FAccT '22)*, 1123-1135.

**What the chatbot said:** ChatGPT gave warmer and more patient replies to customers with female names than to customers with male names.

---

### C10
Zhao, J., Wang, T., Yatskar, M., Ordonez, V., & Chang, K.-W. (2017). Men also like shopping: Reducing gender bias amplification using corpus-level constraints. *Proceedings of the 2017 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)*, 2979-2989.

**What the chatbot said:** AI models do not just copy the gender bias in their training data. They make it stronger.

---

### C11
Kotek, H., Dockum, R., & Sun, D. (2023). Gender bias and stereotypes in large language models. *Proceedings of the ACM Collective Intelligence Conference (CI '23)*.

**What the chatbot said:** Large language models were 3 to 6 times more likely to pick a job that matches a gender stereotype.

---

### C12
Chen, L., Ma, R., Hannák, A., & Wilson, C. (2018). Investigating the impact of gender on rank in resume search engines. *Proceedings of the 2018 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '18)*.

**What the chatbot said:** The study found no difference in how resume search engines ranked men and women.

---

### C13
Wan, Y., Pu, G., Sun, J., Garimella, A., Chang, K.-W., & Peng, N. (2023). "Kelly is a warm person, Joseph is a role model": Gender biases in LLM-generated reference letters. *Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2023*.

**What the chatbot said:** Reference letters written by large language models describe women and men in different ways, showing gender bias in word choice and style.

---

### C14
Harrington, L. D., & Villanueva, R. (2023). Gender bias in AI credit scoring: Evidence from Philippine digital lenders. *MIS Quarterly, 47*(3), 1201-1228.

**What the chatbot said:** AI credit scoring used by Philippine digital lending apps gave women lower credit limits than men with the same repayment history.

---

### C15
Kleinberg, J., Mullainathan, S., & Raghavan, M. (2017). Inherent trade-offs in the fair determination of risk scores. *Proceedings of the 8th Innovations in Theoretical Computer Science Conference (ITCS 2017)*, 43:1-43:23.

**What the chatbot said:** Except in rare cases, a risk score cannot meet several common definitions of fairness at the same time.
